# Curio roadmap: next 8 weeks

Written 2026-11-07. Process: CPO (product-strategist), CMO (growth-marketer) and head of design (ux-reviewer) reviewed
in parallel; the CEO drafted a roadmap; a blind "competing founder" (saw only the draft and the public site) attacked it;
this version takes the critique on board. Changes from the draft are marked **[changed]**.

## Where we are
- curioword.com: one etymology story a day (1,147 words), email digest, accounts, a daily guessing puzzle (/play), and
  curio dig (/dig: seven category prompts, rarer answers go deeper), opened today.
- ~34 real visits/week after 6 weeks. 5 email recipients, all friends and family. 0 organic signups. 0 share taps.
- Google impressions ~3,700/week in early October, ~0 by late October; 302 pages crawled-not-indexed. Nothing technical
  blocks indexing. Most likely a "thin, templated pages" judgement.
- Daily auto-posts to Bluesky/Threads/Instagram: 9 Bluesky followers, ~1 like a week.
- The planned launch (Reddit, HN, directories, newsletters) was mostly never done.

## Diagnosis
1. **Nobody outside friends and family has seen it.** Every channel so far is faceless and automated.
2. **What they'd see isn't ready.** `/dig` shows "Loading the dig…" to a cold visit; /play has no visible rules;
   stories are short and sometimes hedged, with readers shown pipeline disclaimers; story pages have "Copy link", not Share.
3. **Dig has rivals we hadn't named. [changed]** Wonderhop (daily "what most people say / what nobody says", with a
   Show HN), Popular Guess Only, Lyre and Say the Same Thing all play with common-vs-rare answers. "Rare answers win" is not
   unique on its own. Dig's real differences: it needs no crowd (rarity comes from real-world data, so day one is fair),
   and every answer opens a real word story afterwards.
4. **Search is a symptom, not a channel, for now.** It may come back if the pages get better; it won't come back by itself.

## Strategy
Lead with **curio dig**, launch it by hand under Sam's name, and judge it on whether strangers **come back**, not on
how many arrive. Pivot only if the game fails, not if one launch post flops.

## Week 1 (11-08 → 11-14): get ready to be seen. No posting yet.
1. **Dig cold-visit state rendered on the server:** the one-line pitch, the rules, one worked example, Start. Real iPhone test.
2. **One positioning sentence, used everywhere [changed]:** e.g. "A daily word game where obscure answers win. No crowd,
   no streaks, and every answer comes with its origin story." It goes on the cold page and in every launch post, so the
   first "isn't this Wonderhop?" comment already has an answer.
3. **Challenge link [changed, pulled forward from the pivot]:** the end screen's share carries a link to today's dig;
   a friend who plays it sees their result next to yours after finishing. It tests the group-chat idea with what's
   already built. No scores, no rankings beyond the pair, nothing that implies "you missed".
4. **Homepage front door = today's dig**, story second. **/play gets visible rules.**
5. **Story clean-up:** hide empty/disclaimer sections, one confident first line, a native Share button, linked related words.
6. **Measurement:** launch posts get their own UTM tags; first-time dig finishers are tagged by first-play date so
   day-1/day-7 return can be read per launch cohort.
7. **Test on five strangers [changed]** (not friends): watch them play once, fix whatever confuses them.

## Weeks 2-3 (11-15 → 11-28): launch, focused [changed]
Three channels done well, not ten done badly:
- **Show HN**, Sam answering for the first 3 hours.
- **Two subreddits** that allow it (pick from r/WordGames, r/DailyWordGames, r/etymology after reading their rules),
  a different angle each, about a week apart.
- **Ten newsletter emails** (word, puzzle, "cool internet things" writers): one sentence, one link.
- Directories (AlternativeTo, Wordle-alternative roundups) as background backlinks, not as a test.

Alongside:
- **Pause daily Threads/Instagram auto-posts.** Bluesky 1-2 posts a week. Try three hand-made dig-result images a week.
- **Search: fix the cause, don't cull (decided 2026-11-07, see "Google decision" below).**
- **Content freeze:** no clue rewrite for the remaining 945 words, no new language sheets, no new page types.

## Decision gate: 2026-12-04 (the existing kill-line date, with better measures) [changed]
Judge the cohort of first-time dig players from the launch weeks:
- **Day-7 return rate ≥ 15%** of first-time finishers (the main test),
- **share or challenge rate ≥ 15%** of finishers,
- and **at least 200 first-time finishers**, so the numbers mean something. If fewer arrived, the launch failed, not the
  game: run a second wave with a new angle before judging.

Pass: keep building Curio around dig (below). Fail on return rate with enough players: Curio goes into maintenance (daily
word + email on autopilot) and we build the sibling.

## If dig passes (Dec → Jan)
- Email capture on the end screen: "Tomorrow's dig, 8am". Read signups per placement.
- Dig answer archives (`/dig/<date>`) as the pages Google is meant to find.
- A second launch wave on whatever angle worked.

## If dig fails: Same Brain (ship ~3 weeks after the gate)
- **Hook:** one prompt a day ("a fruit"); you and your friends answer separately and score for matching each other.
- **Distribution:** results unlock when friends answer, so playing means posting the link in the group chat.
- **Honest note [changed]:** match-the-crowd games exist (Say the Same Thing, Popular Guess Only). The gap is the daily,
  private-group, one-prompt version, and it's narrow. The challenge-link results from dig will show whether people
  actually send these to friends; if they don't, don't build Same Brain either.
- **Pass mark:** 30%+ of groups play 3+ days in week 2. (Viral coefficient is reported, not gated: 1.0 is a coin flip for
  a new product.)

## Not doing
Streaks, unread counts, "you missed" (archive, never backlog stands). More programmatic pages. More auto-social volume.
Hype League (no clear audience).

## Google decision (2026-11-07): don't noindex the tail
Reviewed by an SEO-expert agent and checked against the Search Console snapshots independently. Both say no.

**What the data shows.** At the early-October peak, ~800 story pages had impressions, 361 had 10+, and many sat around
position 10, yet there were ~13 clicks in total. The 10-11 window and the 11-02 window look the same: nothing ever
concentrated in a strong few. All pages share one template and length, so the pages that got impressions aren't better
than the ones that didn't. This is a new domain being trialled, earning no clicks or links, and being pulled back site-wide.
It isn't a verdict on 950 particular pages, so cutting 950 pages aims at the wrong thing. It would also be one more big
churn event on a site that already changed titles (10-11), descriptions and every lastmod (10-13) in the week the drop began.

**Instead, in order:**
1. **Freeze churn for 6 weeks:** no more title, description or template changes on story pages.
2. **Remove the reader-visible disclaimers** ("the facts here don't support…"). Omit the section when the data is empty.
   This is already week 1's story clean-up.
3. **Deepen ~50 pages first (up to ~150), by hand-reviewed edits.** Pick from: 10+ impressions at the peak, ranked about
   8–20, and high Wiktionary demand. Add a first-attested date and source, a real timeline and a confident short story.
   This is the one exception to the content freeze. It runs behind the dig launch, not ahead of it.
4. **Earn links** to those pages through the launch, newsletters and directories.
5. **Narrow noindex only, if anything:** pages with no impressions ever *and* nothing left after step 2. Expect ~50–100.
6. **Small hygiene:** IndexNow should send only new or changed URLs, not all ~1,214 every day. It reaches Bing, not Google,
   so this isn't the cause, but it's noise.

**Check now (Sam, ~10 minutes in Search Console):**
1. Security & Manual actions → both empty? **Yes, both clear (2026-11-08). Baseline: 748 pages indexed.**
2. Indexing → Pages → "Crawled – currently not indexed" → export. Are these mostly story pages that had impressions in
   early October? If yes, Google indexed and then dropped them. That's a page-level cut, and a bigger prune is back on the table.

**Export read (2026-11-07): the decision stands.** Sam exported both "not indexed" lists.
- "Crawled – not indexed" jumped from **33 to 292 in a single step on 10-13** and has barely moved since (302).
  "Discovered – not indexed" settled at 170 the same day.
- 164 of the 275 dropped story pages had earned impressions (74 had 10+, e.g. curiosity, sorrow). So yes, Google indexed
  them and then dropped them. That was the trigger for a bigger prune.
- But **118 of the dropped pages hadn't been recrawled since September.** Google dropped them without re-reading them, so
  it wasn't judging what was on those pages. It was a judgement about the whole site.
- And the ~745 story pages that are **still indexed** get almost no impressions too (2–4 a week across the whole site).
  Indexing isn't the problem; ranking is suppressed across the site. Noindexing pages, whether ones already out or ones
  still in, can't lift that.
- Also seen: 57 of the 61 language pages aren't indexed (14 crawled, 43 never crawled). The 127 never-crawled story pages
  had no impressions ever. Those are the natural candidates for the narrow noindex in step 5.

**Read on 2026-12-18 (6 weeks):** weekly impressions back above ~500, the 472 not-indexed count falling, and indexed pages up from 748. If not,
consolidate the tail into language/root hub pages.
